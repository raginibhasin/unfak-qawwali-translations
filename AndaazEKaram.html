Unke Andaaz-E-Karam

By Ustad Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan

Penned By Peer Naseeruddin Naseer

Lyrics and Translations

"Unke Andaaz-E-Karam"

"ان کے اندازِ کرم"

( their style / manner of grace / kindness )

Meaning and Interpretation of the Lyrics:

Detailed Interpretation : “Andāaz-E-Karam” refers to the manner in which someone shows compassion — not just kindness itself, but the style and grace of it. In Sufi thought, this can symbolize the Divine grace (Karam) that flows effortlessly from the Beloved (God, or the spiritual beloved who reflects the Divine). It’s not merely about receiving kindness, but being overwhelmed by the beauty of the way it is shown — a kind glance, a forgiving silence, or a tender presence. The Lover (Aashiq) recalls how the Beloved’s mercy manifested — subtle, divine, and humbling. Spiritually, it evokes that moment when the soul feels touched by Divine compassion — where every breath feels like mercy itself.

"Unpe Woh Aana Dil Ka"

"اُن پہ وہ آنا دل کا"

( the heart's being drawn to them )

Meaning and Interpretation of the Lyrics:

Detailed Interpretation : “Dil Kā Aānā” literally means the heart’s arrival — the heart moving toward someone, willingly surrendering. It expresses the state of ishq (love) where the heart doesn’t belong to oneself anymore; it rushes toward the Beloved, drawn by an invisible pull. Emotionally, it signifies the moment of deep attachment or spiritual awakening — when one’s heart found its destination in the Beloved’s presence. In a Sufi sense, this is the moment of divine attraction (Jazb) — when the seeker’s heart is irresistibly pulled toward the Divine Light. It’s both a memory and a yearning-remembering when the heart was alive, devoted, and illuminated by love.

"Haaye Woh Waqt, Woh Baatein, Woh Zamaana Dil Ka"

"ہائے وہ وقت، وہ باتیں، وہ زمانہ دل کا"

( ah, those times, those conversations, that era of the heart )

Meaning and Interpretation of the Lyrics:

Detailed Interpretation : This is a sigh of remembrance — a nostalgic mourning for a lost spiritual era. The poet is reminiscing not just about time, but about a state of being when the heart was alive, full of love, and connected. “Vo bāateiñ” — the conversations — might refer to the intimate dialogues between lover and beloved, or between seeker and the Divine — prayers, whispers, or silent communion. “Vo Zaamāna Dil Kā” — the era of the heart — evokes a time when love ruled over logic, when passion governed life, when the soul felt illuminated. Now, that era seems lost, and the poet sighs — “Haaye” — expressing both pain and gratitude.
Sufi Symbolism : In Sufi poetry, time (Zamāana) often symbolizes states of the heart (Ahwāl) — spiritual stages that come and go. The poet recalls a spiritual peak — perhaps a time of divine nearness — that has now faded into longing. This line beautifully captures “Firaq” (separation after union), which is as sacred as union itself, for it keeps the flame of remembrance alive.

Dil Pe Zakhm Khaate Hain

Lyrics by Sahib Lakhnavi and,
Performed by Ustad Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan

"Dil Pe Zakhm Khaate Hain” unfolds as a meditation on silent suffering — a state where pain is neither complained about nor displayed, but patiently endured. The verses speak from a heart that has learned to carry its wounds with dignity, accepting sorrow as an inseparable part of love and devotion. Here, grief is not weakness; it becomes a form of spiritual strength.

As the qawwali progresses, pain transforms into reflection, and endurance into wisdom. The repeated acceptance of heartbreak suggests a deeper philosophy: that love, in its truest form, is not about fulfillment alone, but about the grace to remain steadfast even when the heart is bruised. Through restrained emotion and layered poetry, the kalaam captures the quiet resilience of a soul that continues to love — despite knowing the cost.

Lyrics and Translations

"Dil Pe Zakhm Khaate Hain, Jaan Se Guzarte Hain,
Jurm Sirf Itna Hai, Unko Pyaar Karte Hain"

"दिल पे जख्म खाते हैं, जान से गुज़रते हैं,
जुर्म सिर्फ़ इतना है, उनको प्यार करते हैं"

دل پر زخم خاتے ہیں، جان سے گزرتے ہیں،

جرم صرف اتنا ہے اُن کو پیار کرتے ہیں

( I endure wounds upon my heart, passing through the edge of life,
the only crime is this—that I love them )

Word Insights
Zakhm (زخم | ज़ख़्म ) : In Urdu poetry, “Zakhm” almost always symbolizes emotional wounds, heartache (especially caused by love, betrayal, or loss) .
Jurm (جرم | जुर्म ) : Something considered wrong by society, fate, or the beloved — often used ironically in love poetry.

"Aitbaar Badhta Hai Aur Bhi Mohabbat Ka,
Jab Woh Ajnabi Bankar Pass Se Guzarte Hain"

"एतबार बढ़ता है और भी मोहब्बत का,
जब वो अजनबी बनकर पास से गुज़रते हैं"

اعتبار بڑھتا ہے اور بھی محبت کا،

جب وہ اجنبی بنا کر پاس سے گزرتے ہیں

( the trust in the love increases even more,
when they pass by me, pretending to be a complete stranger )

Word Insights
Aitbaar ( اعتبار | एतबार ) : To have confidence in someone’s truth, loyalty, or reliability.
Guzarte ( گزرتے | गुज़रते ) : To physically pass from one place to another. In poetry, it can also metaphorically represent the passage of time, life, or emotional distance.

"Unki Anjuman Bhi Hai, Daar Bhi Rassan Bhi Hai,
Dekhna Hai Deewaane Ab Kahaan Theharte Hain"

"उनकी अंजुमन भी है, दार भी रस्सन भी है,
देखना है दीवाने अब कहाँ ठहरते हैं"

اُن کی انجمن بھی ہے، دار بھی رسن بھی ہے،

دیکھنا ہے دیوانے اب کہاں ٹھہرتے ہیں

( their gathering exists, yet so does the gallows and the noose,
now let us see where the mad lovers finally come to rest )

Word Insights
Anjuman ( انجمن | अंजुमन ) : A formal or informal gathering of people. In poetry, the beloved’s social circle or a place where the lover feels judged or ignored.
Daar ( دار | दार ) : A structure used for hanging someone (execution), a symbol of punishment and death. In love poetry, it can represent the emotional torment or the ultimate sacrifice of love.
Rassan ( رسن | रस्सन ) : A rope or noose, often associated with the gallows. In poetry, it can symbolize the constraints of love, the danger of unrequited feelings, or the fine line between devotion and self-destruction.

"Unki Ik Taghaaful Se Toot Te Hain Dil Kitne,
Unki Ek Tawwajjo Se Kitne Zakhm Bharte Hain"

"उनकी इक तग़ाफ़ुल से टूटते हैं दिल कितने,
उनकी एक तवज्जो से कितने ज़ख़्म भरते हैं"

اُن کی اک تغافل سے ٹوٹتے ہیں دل کتنے،

اُن کی اک توجہ سے کتنے زخم بھرتے ہیں

( by a single act of their neglect, countless hearts shatter,
by a single glance of their attention, countless wounds are healed )

Word Insights
Taghaful ( تغافل | तग़ाफ़ुल ) : To knowingly ignore someone, not accidental — but on purpose. In poetry, it represents the pain of being deliberately overlooked by the beloved.
Tawwajjo ( توجہ | तवज्जो ) : To give attention or notice someone. In poetry, it represents the beloved's awareness or care for the lover.

"Jo Pale Hain Zulmat Mein, Kya Sehar Ko Pehchaanein,
Teeragi Ke Shaidaayi Roshni Se Darte Hain"

"जो पले हैं ज़ुलमत में, क्या सहर को पहचानें,
तीरगी के शैदाई रौशनी से डरते हैं"

جو پلے ہیں ظلمت میں کیا سحر کو پہچانیں،

تیرگی کے شیدائی روشنی سے ڈرتے ہیں

( those raised in the darkness, how would they recognize the dawn,
the lovers of the dark [ night ] are frightened by the light )

Word Insights
Zulmat ( ظلمت | ज़ुल्मत ) : Physical darkness; absence of light. In classical poetry, "Zulmat" often represents a state of being lost — internally or spiritually.
Sahar ( سحر | सहर ) : The time just before sunrise. In Urdu poetry, it symbolizes the end of suffering.
Teergi ( تیرگی | तीरगी ) : The state of being in the dark, or the absence of light. In poetry, it often refers to the lover's state of being in darkness, waiting for the beloved's light.
Shaidayi ( شیدائی | शैदाई ) : Someone madly in love or deeply devoted. In poetry, it represents the intense passion and devotion of the lover, often to the point of madness.

"Laakh Woh Gunegaah Hon, Laakh Dushman-E-Jaan Hon,
Dil Ko Kya Karein 'Saahib' Hum Unhi Pe Marte hain"

"लाख वो गुनेगाह हों, लाख दुश्मन-ए-ज़ान हों,
दिल को क्या करें 'साहिब' हम उन्ही पे मरते हैं"

لائک وو گونیگاہ ہوں، لائک دشمن-ای-جان ہوں،

دل کو کیا کریں 'ساحب' ہم انہی پر مرتے ہیں

( even if they are sinners a million times over, even if they are the enemies of my life,
but what can I do with this heart, o 'saahib', I am still devoted only to them )

Word Insights
Gunegaah / Gunahgaar ( گنہگار | गुनहगार ) : One who commits sins or is guilty. In classical Urdu poetry, it often refers to someone who is deeply flawed or has committed transgressions.
Dushman-E-Jaan ( دشمنِ جاں | दुश्मन-ए-जाँ ) : The enemy of one's life or soul. In Urdu poetry, it refers to someone who is the source of pain and suffering in one's life.
